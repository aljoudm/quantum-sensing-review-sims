Maturity ranking of five application domains, based on the 52-source corpus. The primary ranking is the unweighted sum of five scores from 0 to 3 (maximum 15; Table~\ref{tab:loo}): technology readiness level (TRL), field demonstrations, comparison with incumbents, independent groups and commercial availability. The TRL score is not rated by the authors; it follows from the lower bound of the TRL range in Table VI of \cite{r06_Kantsepolsky2026} (TRL 1--3: 0; 4: 1; 5--6: 2; 7 or higher: 3). The other four are consensus scores that the two authors agreed after an independent first rating round. The figure shows a robustness analysis around the primary ranking: $N = 10000$ Monte Carlo draws in which the five criterion weights are sampled from a flat Dirichlet prior and each of the 20 consensus scores is, with probability 0.2, changed by $+1$ or $-1$ with equal chance and clipped to the range 0--3; the TRL score is held fixed. Left: probability of ranking first, counting every tie for first (so the values can sum to more than one). Right: median rank (dot) and 5th to 95th percentile range (line), with tied domains sharing the average rank; rank 1 is the most mature. Domains are listed in the order of the primary ranking; domains with equal sums are ordered by the left-panel probability. The further robustness analyses are leave-one-criterion-out, a rerun with the upper TRL bound, an adversarial test of one-point score changes, and comparisons with the ranking implied by the TRL ranges of \cite{r06_Kantsepolsky2026}; the comparison that excludes TRL as a criterion (domains ranked by the sum of the four judged consensus scores) gives a Spearman rank correlation of 0.92 over the five domains. Reference \cite{r50_Muradoglu2025} is a preprint; no consensus score depends on it, so the primary sums are the same without it (Table~\ref{tab:loo}). The independent first round used the original criterion definitions (the agreed decision rules were adopted for the consensus round only); in that round, agreement between the two authors on the four judged criteria was 55\% exact (11 of 20), with a quadratic-weighted Cohen's $\kappa$ of 0.63 (95\% percentile bootstrap interval 0.31 to 0.88; 10000 resamples of the 20 judged pairs).
