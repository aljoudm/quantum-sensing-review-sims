Robustness of the maturity ranking of five application domains over $N = 10000$ Monte Carlo draws, based on the 52-source corpus. Each domain is scored from 0 to 3 on five criteria: technology readiness level (TRL), field demonstrations, comparison with incumbents, independent groups and commercial availability. The TRL score is not rated by the authors; it follows from the lower bound of the TRL range in Table VI of \cite{r06_Kantsepolsky2026} (TRL 1--3: 0; 4: 1; 5--6: 2; 7 or higher: 3). The other four criteria were scored independently by the two authors from evidence in the corpus, and the consensus score is their mean. In each draw, the five criterion weights are sampled from a flat Dirichlet prior, and each of the 20 consensus scores is, with probability 0.2, changed by $+1$ or $-1$ with equal chance and clipped to the range 0--3; the TRL score is held fixed. Left: probability of ranking first, counting every tie for first (so the values can sum to more than one). Right: median rank (dot) and 5th to 95th percentile range (line), with tied domains sharing the average rank; rank 1 is the most mature. Agreement between raters on the four rated criteria was \textbf{[PERCENT-AGREEMENT]}\% exact, with a quadratic-weighted Cohen's $\kappa$ of \textbf{[KAPPA]} (95\% percentile bootstrap interval \textbf{[KAPPA-CI-LOW]} to \textbf{[KAPPA-CI-HIGH]}; 10000 resamples of the 20 rated pairs). A comparison with the ranking implied by the TRL ranges of \cite{r06_Kantsepolsky2026} that excludes TRL as a criterion (four rated criteria only) gives a Spearman rank correlation of \textbf{[SPEARMAN]}.
