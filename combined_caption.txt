Loss and technical noise bound quantum advantage in practice. (a)~Detected squeezing versus detection efficiency for a pure-loss model, $V_\mathrm{obs}=\eta V_\mathrm{src}+(1-\eta)$, for several source levels; the black curve is the ceiling for infinite source squeezing. Open circle: Advanced LIGO budget, with total efficiency 0.75 and a predicted 4.1~dB from 7.2~dB generated at the source; the pure-loss model reproduces this prediction \cite{r03_Tse2019}. Filled circle: the measured 3.2~dB, for which \cite{r03_Tse2019} infers an efficiency of 0.66 from the same measurement, so agreement there is a consistency check rather than an independent test; the authors attribute the shortfall most likely to higher-than-estimated mode-matching and misalignment loss, and their estimated 15~mrad of phase noise lowers the prediction by only about 0.1~dB. Square: entangled optomechanical probes, with about 4~dB generated, 74\% detection efficiency and about 2.4~dB observed, reported as consistent with the overall loss \cite{r17_Xia2023}. The arrow links budget to measurement; error bars are those of \cite{r03_Tse2019} (smaller than the symbol where not visible). Phase noise and anti-squeezing are not modeled. (b)~Ratio of operating noise to a reference limit: an outdoor gravity gradiometer relative to its estimated quantum projection noise (field test) \cite{r19_Stray2022}; nitrogen-vacancy magnetocardiography in vivo in a magnetically shielded room relative to its shot-noise limit (laboratory) \cite{r22_Arai2022}; and the best reported Rydberg electrometer, about one order of magnitude above the antenna thermal limit as compiled in a review \cite{r04_Kitching2025} (light bar; a classical limit, not directly comparable).
